# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our
community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body
size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender
identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status,
nationality, personal appearance, race, religion, or sexual identity and
orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming,
diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive environment for our
community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologizing to those affected by our mistakes,
  and learning from the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall
  community

Examples of unacceptable behavior include:

* The use of sexualized language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of
  any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address,
  without their explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a
  professional setting

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of
acceptable behavior and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in
response to any behavior that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive,
or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject
comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are
not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation
decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when
an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces.
Examples of representing our community include using an official e-mail address,
posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed
representative at an online or offline event.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior may be
reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement at
**`conduct@chaos-design.example`**.

> inbox before publishing this document.** The domain `chaos-design.example` is
> reserved for documentation and does not receive mail.

You may also contact the maintainers privately through GitHub: for example, use
the private reporting channel in the repository's **Security** tab, or contact
a repository maintainer or organization administrator directly through GitHub's
private messaging. Please do **not** use public Issues, Discussions, pull
requests, or commit messages to report conduct incidents — public reports
expose the reporter and the accused before facts are established.

All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly. All
community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the
reporter of any incident.

## Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining
the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

### 1. Correction

**Community Impact**: Use of inappropriate language or other behavior deemed
unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence**: A private, written warning from community leaders, providing
clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the
behavior was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

Concrete consequences at this level may include: a private written warning that
names the specific behavior and the expected standard; a request to edit or
remove the offending content; a requested apology (public or private depending
on where the harm occurred); and a recorded note that repeated behavior will
escalate to a formal Warning. No participation privileges are removed at this
stage.

### 2. Warning

**Community Impact**: A violation through a single incident or series of
actions.

**Consequence**: A warning with consequences for continued behavior. No
interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with
those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This
includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels
like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent
ban.

Concrete consequences at this level may include: a formal written warning that
becomes part of the moderation record; a defined no-contact period for the
involved parties and enforcement team across community spaces and external
channels; temporary limits on specific privileges (for example, review or
merge rights, or posting in certain spaces); mandatory moderation of future
contributions; and an explicit statement that any further violation will result
in a temporary ban.

### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact**: A serious violation of community standards, including
sustained inappropriate behavior.

**Consequence**: A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public
communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or
private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction
with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period.
Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

Concrete consequences at this level may include: suspension of access to all
community spaces (repository, issues, discussions, chat, events) for a stated
period; removal of maintainer, reviewer, or other privileges for the duration;
removal or hiding of offending contributions where appropriate; a documented
set of conditions for reinstatement (which may include an apology, a
commitment to specific behavior, or a review period); and a ban-evasion policy
in which creating alternate accounts or reappearing elsewhere is treated as an
aggravating factor.

### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact**: Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community
standards, including sustained inappropriate behavior, harassment of an
individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence**: A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the
community.

Concrete consequences at this level may include: permanent removal from all
community spaces and decision-making processes; permanent revocation of all
maintainer and contributor privileges; removal of the individual from
published credits, maintainer lists, and contributor rosters where appropriate;
blocking of alternate accounts used to evade the ban; and a public statement
that may be issued without naming the individual when needed to explain the
removal of their contributions. This is the most serious consequence and is
reserved for patterns of harm or credible threats to participant safety.

### Appeals

A person subject to a Warning, Temporary Ban, or Permanent Ban may request a
review of the decision through a private channel (the reporting address above,
or a GitHub private contact). The review is conducted by maintainers who were
not the primary decision-makers where a sufficient number of maintainers is
available. New evidence, proportionality of the consequence, and consistency
with prior enforcement decisions are considered. The outcome of the review is
communicated privately to the requester; community leaders reserve the final
decision.

## Frequently Asked Questions

The following answers summarize the [official Contributor Covenant FAQ](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/faq)
and adapt it to this project. They are explanatory only and do not modify the
Code of Conduct above.

### How do I report a violation?

Send a report to `conduct@chaos-design.example` (placeholder — see Enforcement)
or contact a maintainer privately through GitHub (the repository's Security tab
private reporting channel, or direct private messaging to a maintainer or
organization admin). Include what happened, where and when, who was involved,
any links or screenshots, and whether the situation is ongoing. You do not need
a polished case or legal language; a concise factual account is enough.

### Can I report anonymously?

Yes. You may withhold your name and use a throwaway email address, or ask a
trusted third party to report on your behalf. Be aware that anonymity limits
our ability to ask follow-up questions, verify details, and tell you the
outcome. GitHub-based private channels may still reveal your account identity
to repository maintainers, so use the address above if you need stronger
anonymity. Reports are handled confidentially to the extent the channel allows.

### What if I am not comfortable reporting to a specific maintainer?

You are not required to report to a particular person. Contact any other
maintainer privately, use the reporting address, or use a GitHub private
channel. If the concern involves the person who would normally handle reports,
say so; the report will be routed to another maintainer who is not the subject
of the complaint.

### How do community leaders stay unbiased when enforcing this Code?

Enforcement decisions are made by the maintainers on the enforcement team.
Where a sufficient number of maintainers is available, a maintainer who is a
party to, or has a close personal relationship with a party to, an incident
recuses themselves from the decision. We apply the same Community Impact
Guidelines to comparable cases, document the reasoning for moderation actions,
keep complainant and respondent identities confidential except as needed to
investigate, and allow decisions to be reviewed by maintainers who were not the
primary decision-makers (see Appeals). Enforcement actions are not decided by
popular vote in a public thread.

### What counts as overreach or a bad-faith report?

Reports must be made in good faith and based on a reasonable belief that the
Code has been violated. Knowingly false reports, reports filed to harass or
silence someone, mass-coordinated false reporting, retaliation against a
reporter or witness, and deliberately misleading evidence are themselves
violations of this Code and may result in the same escalating consequences as
any other violation. Good-faith reports that cannot be substantiated, or that
result in a different outcome than the reporter expected, are not bad faith and
will not be penalized. Concerns about an enforcement decision itself should be
raised through the private Appeals process, not by public harassment of the
decision-makers.

### What happens after I submit a report?

You will receive an acknowledgement (target: within three business days), then
the enforcement team will review the facts, speak with the parties as needed,
and decide whether the Code was violated. Investigations are private and
documented. We aim to communicate a decision or status update within seven
business days for straightforward cases; complex cases take longer, and we will
let you know when that happens. The reporter and the respondent are told the
outcome to the extent permitted by confidentiality and privacy obligations —
typically which guideline level applied and what general action was taken, not
the private details of another person's situation. We do not publish incident
details without the consent of those involved.

### How is community impact considered when deciding consequences?

The four levels in the Enforcement Guidelines are explicitly calibrated by
community impact: a one-off inappropriate remark (Correction), a single
incident or pattern causing real harm (Warning), serious or sustained harmful
behavior (Temporary Ban), and a pattern of harm, targeted harassment, or
aggression toward a class of people (Permanent Ban). Decision-makers consider
the severity and duration of the behavior, whether it was intentional or
reckless, the effect on the people and spaces targeted, prior conduct and any
previous enforcement, and whether the person acknowledged and corrected the
behavior. Intent matters, but impact on the community and on participants'
safety carries at least equal weight; a stated lack of harmful intent does not
erase a real harmful impact. The aim is proportional, consistent action that
protects a welcoming and safe community, not punishment for its own sake.
